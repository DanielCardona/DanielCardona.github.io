Spaced repetition: what it is and how to use it

Updated: 2026-10-20 · 5 min read

Spaced repetition means reviewing information at increasingly longer time intervals. It is one of the best-supported study techniques in memory research and fits vocabulary learning perfectly.

The forgetting curve

In the late 19th century, psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus studied how we forget what we memorize: forgetting is fast at first and slows down over time. Each time you recall a memory just before it fades, it becomes firmer and takes longer to forget. That is the idea behind the "spacing effect": spreading out your reviews works better than cramming them together.

An example schedule

There is no single rule; this is a rough guide:

  1. First review: the same day or the next.
  2. Second review: after 3 days.
  3. Third: after a week.
  4. Fourth: after 2-3 weeks.
  5. Later ones: more and more spread out (a month, several months).

If you miss a word, bring it closer again: review it tomorrow and restart the ladder of intervals.

How to apply it without special tools

  • Leitner boxes: use several boxes (or folders). New words start in box 1 and are reviewed daily; if you get one right it moves up to the next box, reviewed less often; if you miss it, it goes back to box 1.
  • A spreadsheet with the next review date for each word.
  • Apps that schedule reviews for you.

Getting the most out of it

  • Recall before looking: the effort of retrieving the word is what strengthens the memory.
  • Small cards: one idea or word per card.
  • Be consistent: reviews only work if you do them; a few minutes a day.
  • Do not build up debt: if you skip a week, ease back in instead of trying to catch up in one day.
  • Drop what is not useful: if a word is not useful to you, remove it.
